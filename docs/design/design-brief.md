# Prompt for Fable — Dhruva Visual Design Exploration

I'm building Dhruva, a personal cognitive-clarity companion app — an AI that tells me the one thing that matters right now and gives me permission to ignore everything else. I need visual design direction: color palette, typography, and a distinct visual language. I have the product philosophy locked but zero visual identity yet.

## What Dhruva is NOT (design implications)

- **Not a productivity app.** No dashboards, no charts, no progress bars, no streak counters, no percentage-complete anything. If a design pattern reminds me of Notion, Todoist, Linear, or a fitness tracker, it's wrong.
- **Not cheerful or gamified.** No confetti, no badges, no "you're crushing it" energy. The tone is a calm, competent colleague — not a cheerleader, not a therapist.
- **Not data-dense.** The product shows at most 3 items anywhere, ever. Most screens show exactly one thing.

## The core interaction

The whole app is essentially: a chat interface, one "Now" card showing the single current directive, and a weekly review. That's it — three screens. Someone opening the app should feel like checking in with a trusted person, not opening a tool.

## Emotional register to hit

Think: a lighthouse keeper's logbook. A pole star (Dhruva's namesake — "the one fixed point in a turning sky"). Steady, quiet authority. Something that feels *permanent* and *unhurried* — the opposite of a notification-red, urgency-driven app. Warmth expressed through restraint, not through bubbly color or mascots.

Reference feelings, not reference apps: the calm of a well-kept notebook, the quiet confidence of an old analog instrument (compass, sundial), night-sky/pole-star imagery used sparingly as a motif rather than literally.

## What I need from you

1. **2–3 distinct visual directions** (mood boards or descriptions), each with a one-line rationale for why it fits the "pole star / trusted colleague" feeling rather than a productivity-app feeling.
2. For your favorite direction: a color palette (with hex codes) and typography pairing (headline + body), explained in terms of the emotional register above — not just "looks modern."
3. Applied to these five real screen states, so I can see it in context, not just as a swatch:
   - **Chat / Now card** — a chat thread with one directive card pinned at the top (e.g. "Call PF office — 10 min. Everything else is not your problem until 11am.")
   - **Weekly Review** — a short scrollable narrative (five short sections: Witness, Shape, One Pattern, Hospice, Star), no charts
   - **Focus Block active state** — full-screen, minimal, showing only the current task and a countdown, designed to feel like a held boundary rather than a timer app
   - **Hospice modal** — a gentle "these 6 things haven't moved in a month, keep or release?" screen — needs to feel like relief, not failure
   - **Empty / Quiet state** — the screen when nothing needs attention ("Nothing needs you right now. That's real.") — this might be the most important screen in the whole app; it should feel earned and peaceful, not blank or broken.
4. Note any places where a "calm" palette risks looking inert or low-signal on a phone screen, and how you'd solve that without reaching for typical app-urgency colors (red badges, bright orange alerts).

## Constraints

- Mobile-first (Android primary, iOS secondary) — this needs to work as a native app screen, not a desktop web layout.
- Dark mode and light mode both matter — a lot of use happens first thing in the morning and I want to know which one you'd lead with and why.
- No literal iconography of the pole star or stars unless it's genuinely subtle — I don't want it to read as a "space" app.
